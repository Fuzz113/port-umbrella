01 Modelling · Simulation · Risk Ongoing

Autonomous VTOL delivery UAS

A currently ongoing individual project: a level 4 autonomous aircraft that takes off vertically, searches a field for a landing pad, delivers a parcel and flies home. The requirements, design and full mission simulation are done. The build and test flight are planned for April 2027, with the eventual goal of delivering the entire system.

A model built from first principles

Before any hardware exists, the whole mission runs in a Simulink model I built: six degree of freedom flight dynamics, atmosphere and turbulence, battery energy, sensors and a camera, with the decision logic written in Stateflow.

I then tried to break it. Twenty-one scenarios cover wind, an aged battery, GNSS jamming, sensor faults, crossing traffic and obstacles. Each run is scored automatically against pass and fail criteria and logs around 37 metrics for analysis.

The failures were the useful part. Each one traced back to a design assumption, and each one changed a requirement.

Scenario testing

Stress the model, not just the happy path

18 of 21 scenarios pass. The three that fail are genuine findings, and each became a requirement change.

Decisions under uncertainty

A reserve rule that adapts

A fixed "return at 35%" rule ran an aged battery flat. I replaced it with an energy-aware rule holding a 1.3x margin and a 15% reserve.

Risk analysis

Root causes before fixes

P-diagram, Kepner-Tregoe problem definition and Ishikawa fishbone analysis across six failure modes, to ARP4761A principles.

Traceability

Every number has a source

107 requirements following the V-Model, modelled in SysML and linked to model elements with no unresolved links.

Logic and architecture

From model to hardware

A Cube Orange+ flies the aircraft and a Raspberry Pi 5 plans the search and processes the camera images. An independent flight termination system sits between the flight controller and all five motor controllers. It shares no sensor, radio, processor or battery with the rest of the aircraft.

The avionics live in a 3D printable pod: 11 ASA parts, 448 g, 498 mm long, sliced and ready to print.

Stage 1 · Year 3 group project

Trim Reaper

Project Manager and Lead Structural Engineer · team of 9

The brief: carry up to 4 kg while weighing as little as possible, across three flights with no changes in between. I managed the team and led the structures work, from first sketches through design reviews to the flying prototype.

  • Rib and spar wing with I-beam spars, box fuselage and V-tail, with full technical drawings
  • Hand calculations for shear, bending, stress and deflection, checked against ANSYS FEA at 6.3 kg take-off weight
  • Practical load tests to CS-VLA factors, and V-n diagrams with gust loads
  • Laser-cut balsa and ply from nested layouts to cut waste

Flew in March 2026 and lifted about 3.5 kg in testing.

Stage 2 · Team Phoenix, extracurricular

Phoenix

Lead Structural Engineer

We carried the prototype forward as a competition entry. Most of the work was structural: taking weight out without losing the strength the payload flights needed.

  • Wingspan cut from 2 m to 1.75 m, with the length scaled to match
  • Tail made slightly smaller to calm the twitchy handling seen on Trim Reaper
  • Fuselage redesigned to save weight
  • New airframe built and covered in the university workshop

Flew at the BMFA Payload Challenge in June 2026 and placed 2nd.

Avionics Systems · 2026

AICDS: attitude indicator and collision detection

An avionics system with a MATLAB-based GUI, developed requirement-first through the V-Model. I modelled the architecture in Simulink, wrote requirements at three levels with full traceability, and used a Kalman filter to turn noisy sensor data into a steady attitude display.

MATLAB · Simulink · Kalman filter · 92 requirements, each with a test case

Computational Fluid Dynamics · 2025

Mach 2.5 interceptor wing

A wing sized from an operational requirement and proven in 2D and 3D CFD. Structured meshes with a mesh independence study, validated against published aerofoil data before any conclusions were drawn.

ANSYS Fluent · validation against published data · Mach 2.5 at 18 km
